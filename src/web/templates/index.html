How to Read the Cash Map & 4 Solvency Quadrants (Investor Guide)
→ X-Axis: Cash Coverage Ratio (Cash Buffer)

Measures how much real cash sits in bank accounts relative to interest-bearing debt maturing within the year:

  • Right (≥ 1.0x / Debt-Free): Strong and safe cash reserves, able to cover all current-year short-term debt obligations.
  • Left (< 0.20x): Very thin cash (less than 20% of short-term debt), facing tight liquidity headroom.
↑ Y-Axis: Sloan Accrual Ratio (Earnings Quality)

Tests earnings integrity | whether reported profit is backed by actual operating cash inflow or exists mostly on paper:

  • Bottom (≤ 0%): High-quality earnings backed by real operating cash (positive CFO). Real cash entering the bank.
  • Top (> +10%): Accrual-heavy profit. Cash has not yet arrived as it remains tied up in project receivables.
Dashed Blue Line (Sector Linear Regression)

Represents the industry equilibrium line. Issuers above the red line (+1.5σ) show unusual receivable buildup; issuers below the green line (-1.5σ) generate cash flow above sector peers.

Note: Use the sector filter buttons above because each sector (Construction, FMCG, Banking, Energy) has a distinct cash conversion cycle.
4 Practical Solvency Quadrants
Bottom-Right Cash Fortress: Thick cash, real cash-backed earnings (Safest).
Top-Left Double Stress: Thin cash, paper-only earnings (High liquidity risk).
Bottom-Left Pure Cash Stress: Thin cash, though earnings are not accrual-inflated.
Top-Right Safe Cash, Paper Profit: Cash is adequate, but monitor receivable collection.
